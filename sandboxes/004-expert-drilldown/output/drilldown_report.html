Unversioned Rate Manual Reference — Other-State Insured Locations

Source: Kentucky National Insurance Company Homeowners Policy Program Manual (KY-SERFF-KNIC-127064322)
Section 602 — Other Insured Location Occupied by Insured
Verbatim triggering language
If the insured location is in another state, refer to the Manual for that state.
Full section context: "Section II Coverage may be provided on locations, other than the residence premises, where an insured resides, but which are insured for Section I Coverage under another insurance program or by another company. Make the appropriate charge for each other insured location shown in the Declarations of this policy. If the insured location is in another state, refer to the Manual for that state."
The gap

The rate rule directs the adjuster to "the Manual for that state" with no edition, version date, effective date, or filing identifier. The same manual's Section 204 explicitly acknowledges that manual revisions occur and take effect "as announced" — meaning the referenced Manual can change. There is no anchor that determines which version of the Manual governed a specific policy at issuance or a specific claim at loss date. If the Manual is revised between policy issuance and the date of loss, neither the carrier nor the policyholder has a mechanism to establish which version applied.

⚖️ Compliance & Coverage Counsel
KRS 304.13-051 Requires every insurer to file with the commissioner rates and supplementary rate information used in Kentucky. Filings must be complete. A rate rule that references an unidentified external document does not constitute a complete, verifiable filing.
806 KAR 13:150 — P&C Rate and Rule Filings, § 10 An insurer adopting loss costs of an advisory organization must identify the specific filing being adopted. The same principle of traceability applies to any external manual reference: the filing must point to a specific, identified document.
806 KAR 13:110 — Rate Standards for P&C Flex Rating Manual rates, rating schedules, and rating plans shall be made and adopted in accordance with filed and approved rate standards. A floating reference to an unversioned manual is not a rate standard that can be verified against the filed and approved record.
KNIC Manual Section 204 — Manual Premium Revision The manual's own revision provision states revisions take effect "as announced" and may apply to renewals. This confirms the Manual is subject to change — making the unanchored reference in Section 602 a live versioning gap, not a static reference.
Which specific edition and effective date of the referenced Manual was in force on the policy effective date? If the Manual has been revised since issuance, which version governs this claim — and where is that documented?
📋 Claims Professional

When a claim arises for a secondary location in another state, the adjuster must calculate the Section II charge using "the Manual for that state." But which edition? The one in force at policy issuance, at loss date, or at claim filing? The filing provides no answer.

Dispute scenario: An insured suffers a liability loss at a secondary residence in another state. The adjuster applies the current Manual. The policy was issued two years earlier under a prior Manual edition with a different rate schedule. The insured's attorney requests production of the Manual version that governed the policy at issuance. The carrier cannot produce contemporaneous documentation of which version applied. The policyholder has no independent access to the Manual and cannot verify the calculation.
Bad-faith exposure: MEDIUM

Coverage is not denied outright. The risk is that the calculation is unverifiable. Kentucky courts construe policy ambiguity against the drafter. A floating reference to an unversioned external document is a form of policy ambiguity. If the carrier applies a revised Manual less favorable than the one in force at issuance, the policyholder has no basis to identify or challenge it.

✏️ Policy Designer / Filing Specialist
Current language
If the insured location is in another state, refer to the Manual for that state.
Suggested language
If the insured location is in another state, refer to the Kentucky National Insurance Company Homeowners Policy Program Manual applicable to that state, in the edition in effect on the policy effective date. The applicable edition and SERFF filing reference shall be identified in the Declarations or rate supplement accompanying this policy.
DOI refiling required: Yes. This is a supplementary rating information change under KRS 304.13-051 and 806 KAR 13:150. File via SERFF under the existing TOI (04.0 / 04.1) with a letter of explanation per 806 KAR 14:006 § 6 describing the change and confirming it does not alter the underlying rate structure.
Note: The language fix alone is not sufficient. The policy package must also include documentation of which Manual edition governed at issuance — either in the Declarations or as a rate supplement. Without that, a future revision still leaves the adjuster without a dated anchor.

Rate Methodology Without Regulatory Citation — 5 Findings Across Both Carriers

Primary instance: KFBM Underwriting Manual (KY-SERFF-KFBM-134870230-UW-MANUAL) · Also: KFBM Rate Manual HOP1/HOP2/HOP8 · KNIC Rate Information
Sections: Homeowner Risk Factor · Roof Rating Factor · Deductible Factor · Base Premium Computation · Rate Information
Primary verbatim instance — Homeowner Risk Factor (KFBM)
The Homeowner Risk Factor is a rating component made up of data derived from claim experience and a Property Insurance Score. The factor will be calculated at new business, reinstatements with a lapse and renewals.
Supporting instances: (1) "The Roof Rating Factor is a rating component based on the age of the dwelling's roof. The factor will be calculated at new business, reinstatements with a lapse and renewals." — KFBM HOP8. (2) Deductible Factor interpolation formula across Coverage A bounds — KFBM HOP2. (3) "Rate data applies to filing. Filing Method: Use & File. Rate Change Type: Increase." — KNIC Rate Information.
The gap

Both carriers' rate-setting filings contain rating methodology sections — base premium computation, homeowner risk factors, roof rating factors, deductible factors — with no traceable citation to the Kentucky statute, administrative regulation, or DOI bulletin that authorizes each methodology. The Homeowner Risk Factor is the most significant instance: it incorporates a "Property Insurance Score" derived from an external third-party source, with no identification of that source and no citation to the regulatory authority permitting its use in Kentucky homeowners rating. A market conduct examiner cannot verify from these filings alone that each rating component is authorized under Kentucky law. This pattern appears independently in both carriers — it is an industry-wide drafting convention, not an isolated filing choice.

⚖️ Compliance & Coverage Counsel
KRS 304.13-050 Rates shall not be excessive, inadequate, or unfairly discriminatory. A filing that does not cite the regulatory basis for a rating component cannot demonstrate compliance with this standard from the face of the filing alone.
KRS 304.13-051 Requires every insurer to file rates and supplementary rate information with the commissioner. A methodology filing without a regulatory citation is incomplete as a matter of verifiability — the DOI cannot confirm the methodology is authorized without additional investigation beyond the filing itself.
806 KAR 13:110 — Rate Standards, § 1 Manual rates and rating plans shall be made and adopted in accordance with sound actuarial principles. A rating component that operates without a cited regulatory basis cannot be independently verified as meeting this standard during a market conduct examination.
KRS 304.20-040 — Unfair Discrimination (verify) Prohibits unfair discrimination between insureds of the same class. The use of a "Property Insurance Score" — a composite score from a third-party data source — may implicate this provision. Verify whether Kentucky has adopted NAIC Model #759 guidance on use of credit information in personal lines rating before relying on this citation.
For each rating component: what Kentucky statute or administrative regulation authorizes this specific methodology? For the Property Insurance Score: who provides it, what data does it incorporate, and what regulatory authority permits its use in Kentucky homeowners rating?
📋 Claims Professional

These are primarily rate-layer findings, not claims-layer. The direct claims exposure is limited. The regulatory and market-conduct exposure is material.

Regulatory scenario: A Kentucky DOI market conduct examiner reviews KFBM's rate filing following a consumer complaint about a premium surcharge. The examiner asks: "On what regulatory authority does KFBM rely for the Homeowner Risk Factor?" The filing contains no citation. KFBM must reconstruct its regulatory basis during the exam — a position that invites a non-compliance finding regardless of whether the underlying methodology is actuarially sound.
Individual claim risk: LOW Market conduct risk: HIGH

Downstream claims exposure arises if a rating factor is found unauthorized post-issuance: ordered premium refunds across all policyholders rated under that factor. If the Property Insurance Score is found to constitute unfair discrimination, affected policyholders may have grounds for regulatory complaints and bad-faith claims based on the rating decision.

Industry-wide signal: both carriers independently show the same gap. This is a systemic drafting convention, not an isolated error.

  • 3. Base Premium Computation — verify full section carries citations.
  • 4. Deductible Factor — interpolation formula; cite the filed rate schedule that authorizes the factor table.
DOI refiling required: Yes. Adding regulatory citations constitutes supplementary rating information changes under KRS 304.13-051 and 806 KAR 13:150. If the Property Insurance Score provider is being identified for the first time, this may require a new rate filing rather than a supplementary amendment — verify with DOI before filing.

Undisclosed ACV Calculation Methodology — Roof Surfacing Loss Settlement

Primary: KY-SERFF-KFBM-134503212-HO-FORM (KFBM HO 04 93, 2025) · Supporting: KY-SERFF-KNIC-127064322 (KNIC Rate/Rule)
KFBM: SECTION I — CONDITIONS · KNIC: Section 406 — Additional Amounts of Insurance Form HO 00 03
Primary Verbatim Evidence — KFBM HO 04 93, Section I Conditions
"Such loss will be subject to actual cash value loss settlement. Therefore, the loss settlement conditions that pertain to 'repair or replacement cost without deduction for depreciation' are changed as noted below: In Form HO 00 03 and HO 00 02, Condition 3. Loss Settlement..."
Context: Applied when the entire roof surfacing has not been replaced within 7 years of the loss date. Endorsed separately as "Actual Cash Value Loss Settlement — Windstorm or Hail Losses to Roof Surfacing." Supporting KNIC instance (Section 406): "The policy provides loss settlement for buildings insured under Coverage A or B on a replacement cost basis without deduction for depreciation, if, at the time of loss, the amount of insurance on the damaged building..." — references ISO HO 00 03 conditions with no methodology citation.
Gap Statement

KFBM's proprietary base form HO 04 93 imposes ACV loss settlement on roof surfacing when the roof has not been entirely replaced within 7 years of the loss date. The form uses "actual cash value" as the operative settlement standard without defining the calculation methodology anywhere in the filed policy package. No depreciation schedule, depreciation tool, or methodology basis — including whether labor costs are depreciated — is disclosed. A policyholder with a roof older than 7 years who suffers a windstorm or hail loss will have their claim settled on an ACV basis they cannot predict, verify, or challenge from the policy documents alone. Kentucky sits within a high-frequency hail and wind corridor; this restriction affects a majority of the active book. ISO HO 00 03 defines ACV but does not specify the depreciation methodology applied at claim time — a gap established by over a decade of labor depreciation class action litigation nationwide. The same methodology gap appears in KNIC's filing (Section 406), which references replacement cost settlement under HO 00 03 without anchoring the calculation standard.

⚖️ Compliance / Counsel
KRS 304.12-230 — Unfair Competition and Deceptive Practices Prohibits misrepresentation of the benefits, advantages, conditions, or terms of any insurance policy. A loss settlement provision that applies ACV without disclosing the calculation methodology may constitute a misrepresentation of the actual settlement conditions the policyholder will face.
KRS 304.20-040 — Unfair Claims Settlement Practices Prohibits failing to adopt and implement reasonable standards for prompt, fair, and equitable settlements. A carrier that applies an undisclosed depreciation methodology — particularly one that depreciates labor costs — cannot demonstrate that its settlement standard is disclosed to or verifiable by the policyholder.
806 KAR 12:095 — Homeowners Insurance Establishes minimum standards for homeowners policy form content in Kentucky. A policy form that triggers ACV loss settlement without defining the ACV calculation methodology does not provide the policyholder with sufficient information to understand the terms of their coverage.
806 KAR 20:010 — Unfair Claims Settlement Practices Prohibits requiring an insured to submit to a less favorable settlement standard than disclosed in the policy. An undisclosed depreciation methodology that reduces a settlement below what the policyholder reasonably expected creates exposure under this standard.
What methodology does KFBM use to calculate ACV for roof surfacing losses? Is labor depreciated? If so, under what schedule and on what regulatory authority? Has this methodology been filed with and approved by the Kentucky DOI? Can a market conduct examiner reproduce a sample ACV calculation from the filed policy documents alone?
🗂️ Claims Professional

Kentucky is in a high-frequency hail and wind corridor. Windstorm and hail are the most common homeowners claim types in the state. KFBM's 7-year roof rule means that any policyholder with a roof older than 7 years — a substantial majority of the book — who suffers a windstorm or hail loss receives ACV rather than replacement cost on roof surfacing. The ACV calculation methodology is not disclosed in the policy. If KFBM depreciates labor costs as part of its ACV calculation, that undisclosed methodology creates direct bad-faith exposure for every such claim in the book.

Dispute scenario: A Kentucky homeowner with a 10-year-old roof suffers hail damage. KFBM pays ACV on the roof surfacing claim using an internal depreciation schedule that depreciates both materials and labor. The policyholder receives a settlement materially less than the repair cost. The policyholder asks how the number was calculated; KFBM cannot point to any policy document disclosing the methodology. Counsel identifies the same pattern litigated in Henn v. American Family (Iowa, 2016), Wilcox v. State Farm (multiple states, 2016–2019), and related class actions. A class-wide claim is filed on behalf of all KFBM policyholders with roofs older than 7 years who received ACV roof settlements in the past five years.
Bad-Faith Risk: HIGH

Affects high-frequency claim type (wind/hail) across large share of active book. Labor depreciation class-action pattern is established. Kentucky applies broad bad-faith standard under KRS 304.12-230.

🛠️ Policy Designer

Current language:

"Such loss will be subject to actual cash value loss settlement."

Suggested redline:

"Such loss will be subject to actual cash value loss settlement. For purposes of this provision, actual cash value means the cost to repair or replace the damaged property with material of like kind and quality, less depreciation. Depreciation shall be calculated using [carrier's named depreciation schedule or tool, filed with the Kentucky DOI under SERFF tracking number [X], effective [date]]. Labor costs [are / are not] subject to depreciation under this provision. The depreciation methodology applied to any loss under this provision will be disclosed to the insured upon request within [X] business days of the initial settlement offer."

Three elements are required: (1) define ACV explicitly in the provision that triggers it; (2) identify the depreciation tool or schedule by name and filing reference; (3) state clearly whether labor is depreciated. If KFBM does not depreciate labor, stating that explicitly is both a competitive advantage and a liability shield. Amending loss settlement conditions requires a form filing under 806 KAR 14:006. If a new depreciation schedule is being identified for the first time, supplementary rate information filing under KRS 304.13-051 may also be required.
