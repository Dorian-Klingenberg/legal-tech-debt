# Accessibility map for Kentucky homeowners policy filing packages

## Core findings

The highest-friction part of your document universe is **KFBM’s proprietary homeowners package**, especially the **base jacket** and any foundational filings that predate **November 1, 2018**, because Kentucky’s public SERFF Filing Access only exposes filings made after that date. Kentucky DOI’s own public-access guidance says post-2018 electronic filings are available in SERFF Filing Access at no cost, while filings before November 1, 2018 must be obtained from the Department through open records. Kentucky also warns that SERFF Filing Access is only an alternative access method, that the state decides what is public, and that users must observe third-party copyrights; the NAIC terms also prohibit bypassing the intended workflow to automate downloads. For an automated gap-detector project, that means the cleanest acquisition path is **one written DOI request for current in-force packages plus the foundational pre-2018 approving filings**, rather than trying to scrape SFA. citeturn12view0turn11search4turn15search4

The lowest-friction part of your universe is **KNIC’s current manuals and underwriting rules**. KNIC publicly hosts a current Kentucky homeowners manual and underwriting guidelines on its own website. The manual expressly shows that KNIC writes homeowners on ISO base forms **HO 00 03, HO 00 05, HO 00 04, HO 00 06, and HO 00 08**, lists a long menu of HO endorsements and KNIC-specific PCH/PCO/PCA forms, and identifies several endorsements that must be used with all Kentucky homeowners policies. That is unusually valuable for referential-integrity work because it gives you a public, carrier-hosted map of many mandatory and optional attachments without any DOI interaction. citeturn26view0turn27view0turn30view0turn32view0

For **ISO bureau forms**, the practical issue is not just public-record access. It is also **copyright**. The Kentucky SERFF public-access terms say users must observe all copyrights associated with materials accessed through the interface and that users may be subject to enforcement by third parties. ISO-form copies found on public portals or in DOI responses therefore may still carry use restrictions separate from the open-records question. In practice, that makes public state portals and legacy samples good for **reference and audit**, but not a clean substitute for a licensed ISO text source if your eventual use goes beyond internal compliance review. citeturn12view0turn37search0

Kentucky’s open-records process itself is straightforward, but there are two real friction points. First, the request must be **written**; a phone call is useful for pre-flighting scope, but not as the operative request. Second, Kentucky’s Open Records Act is framed around **Kentucky residents’** inspection rights, and agencies may ask requesters to state how they qualify as Kentucky residents. Agencies must give an initial written response within **five business days**, but production may occur later if the agency explains the delay. For noncommercial requests, copying fees may not exceed actual reproduction cost, and Kentucky guidance says **ten cents per page** is generally reasonable for paper copies; if the request is deemed **commercial**, the agency may recover staff time too. Because your use case may be part of a for-profit workflow, that commercial-purpose issue is a genuine procurement-risk variable. citeturn7view2turn46view0turn43view0turn7view1

## Kentucky open-records mechanics

Kentucky DOI falls under the **Public Protection Cabinet** open-records policy. The policy identifies the official custodian for the **Department of Insurance** as: **Attn: Records Custodian, 500 Mero Street, 2nd Floor, Frankfort, KY 40601; phone 502-564-3630; email doi.openrecordsrequest@ky.gov**. The same policy says requests must be made **in writing** and may be submitted by **U.S. mail, hand delivery, electronic message, or the PPC online portal**. Kentucky’s statutes separately say an agency cannot require any particular form, though it may accept the Attorney General’s standard form. In other words: the **portal, email, or letter** are all valid; the portal is not mandatory. citeturn46view0turn7view2

The governing legal framework is **KRS Chapter 61**, especially **KRS 61.872** on the right to inspect public records and request methods, **KRS 61.874** on copies and fees, and **KRS 61.878** on exemptions, together with **200 KAR 1:020**, Kentucky’s uniform regulation on access to public records. Kentucky’s Attorney General guide confirms the operational rules that matter most here: agencies must respond within five business days, better-crafted requests should identify **specific existing records** rather than broad topics, agencies are not required to create records or compile new information, and they may reject requests that impose an **unreasonable burden**. citeturn7view2turn7view1turn8view0turn7view0turn43view0

For cost, the best strategy is to request **existing electronic PDFs in their maintained electronic format**. Kentucky law says nonexempt public records used for noncommercial purposes must be available in standard electronic format if the agency maintains them electronically, and the AG guide says copying fees cannot exceed actual reproduction costs and generally **ten cents per page** is considered reasonable for paper copying. The guide also makes clear that agencies may not charge ordinary staff time on a standard noncommercial request, but they **may** recover staff time on **commercial-purpose** requests, and they may recover staff costs if you demand a nonstandard customized format. Since SERFF filings are already electronic, asking for electronic delivery is the lowest-cost route. citeturn7view1turn43view0

The main Kentucky exemption that matters for form filings is the proprietary/confidential-record exemption in **KRS 61.878(1)(c)**, which covers records confidentially disclosed to an agency and generally recognized as confidential or proprietary where disclosure would create unfair commercial advantage, including records maintained in conjunction with regulation of a commercial enterprise or review of a license to do business. That is the clearest statutory basis for redacting manuals, rating logic, or proprietary forms. **Copyright**, by contrast, does not appear in KRS 61.878 as a standalone exemption category; on the sources I found, it looks more like a **downstream use/republication issue** than a clean Kentucky open-records withholding ground. Kentucky’s SFA terms reinforce that distinction by saying users must observe copyrights and may face third-party enforcement even when the state has made a filing public. citeturn8view0turn12view0

One more friction point is requester status. **KRS 61.872** says public records are open for inspection by any **resident of the Commonwealth**, and allows agencies to require the requester to state how they qualify as a Kentucky resident. If you are not making the request as a Kentucky resident or through a Kentucky entity, counsel, employee, or affiliate, confirm that point before you submit. citeturn7view2

Kentucky DOI says SFA only exposes filings made **after November 1, 2018**, while older filings must be requested from DOI. Because your target base jacket predates that cutoff, the jacket is **not reliably procurable through Kentucky’s public SFA alone**. That makes the base jacket a **write-request item by default**, especially because the form number is presently unknown. citeturn12view0

For **KFBM current rate and underwriting manuals**, your own note that you already have some post-2018 filings fits the official Kentucky access model: some later manuals or manual-page changes may indeed be visible in SFA, but older foundations and any nonpublic redacted elements will require DOI production. Because I did not find a public KFBM manual library comparable to KNIC’s, the safest assumption is that KFBM manuals are a **mixed-source set**: partly public through post-2018 SFA, partly obtainable only from DOI, and possibly partially redactable if KFBM invoked proprietary treatment under Kentucky’s exemption rules. citeturn12view0turn20view1turn8view0

## ISO forms and no-interaction substitutes

For **ISO HO 00 03, HO 00 05, and the Kentucky HO 04-series library**, the formal public-access path is still state-based. The NAIC describes SERFF Filing Access as a tool that lets the public view filings that a **participating state has marked available**, and Kentucky’s SFA terms confirm that the state, not NAIC, decides what is public. So yes, ISO filings are the kind of thing that can appear in SFA, but visibility is **state-specific and filing-specific**, not universally guaranteed. In this session I could not directly inspect Kentucky SFA filing-detail pages for each ISO filing, so I cannot responsibly tell you that every Kentucky ISO HO filing you want is publicly downloadable there today. citeturn35search0turn12view0

On **copyright**, the practical answer is “yes, separate issue.” Kentucky’s SERFF public-access terms warn that users must observe copyrights associated with materials accessed through the interface, and that a user may be subject to enforcement by a third party. That means even if Kentucky DOI or another DOI portal releases an ISO form, that does **not** mean ISO’s copyright evaporates. The safest operational distinction is this: **public-record release answers access; copyright answers reuse**. For internal audit, comparison, and referential-integrity review, public copies are still highly useful. For product distribution, republication, or commercial redistribution, a licensed source is safer. citeturn12view0turn37search0

Your best **no-interaction substitutes** for ISO text are these:

Kentucky is not the only state exposing public filings through SFA. Official insurance-department pages in **Nevada, Washington, Wisconsin, Indiana, Michigan, and Arizona** all direct the public to SFA or a public filing search for downloading public rate/form filings. Wisconsin additionally says that if a filing predates October 15, 2015, users should use OCI’s older Policy Forms and Rate Filings Search. Nevada says it authorizes public access to **non-proprietary** filing elements after review. These are useful for obtaining clean ISO benchmark text when Kentucky access is missing or redacted, but they do **not** prove Kentucky approval or Kentucky-specific amendatories. citeturn35search1turn35search2turn35search3turn36search0turn36search1turn35search19turn35search4

That is useful as a legacy baseline and a quick semantic scaffold, but it is not a current Kentucky approval record and it is not the same thing as a current-edition Kentucky-specific filed form with amendatories. During this pass, I did **not** find evidence that III maintains a public library of current ISO homeowners forms comparable to what you need. citeturn37search0turn37search1

I did **not** find a public NAIC repository of carrier-approved policy forms across states. ## One-request acquisition strategy

If you want to maximize yield in a **single interaction** with Kentucky DOI, the most efficient path is **call first, write immediately after**. The call to **502-564-3630** should be used to confirm the custodian, preferred delivery method, and whether DOI would prefer one consolidated request or one email with clearly labeled subparts. But the legally operative request still needs to be **written**, and the best direct written channel is **doi.openrecordsrequest@ky.gov** or the PPC online portal. citeturn46view0turn7view2

The key is to avoid an “any and all records” request. Kentucky guidance specifically warns that requests should identify **specific existing records** and a **defined time period**, and that broad subject-matter requests can be denied as unreasonably burdensome. For your use case, the right framing is not “send all homeowners filings ever made,” but rather: **send the records necessary to identify the currently approved and currently in-use Kentucky homeowners package, plus the first approving filing and latest amending filing for each currently used base form, endorsement, and manual set**. That is narrower, more defensible, and much more likely to survive burden objections. citeturn43view0turn7view2

A strong single request would ask DOI for:

- **KFBM**: the currently approved and currently in-use Kentucky homeowners **base policy jacket**, all endorsements that attach to a standard owner-occupied homeowners policy, the current rate/rule manual pages, the current underwriting manual or underwriting guidelines, and the **first approving filing plus any later amending/replacement filings** for the base jacket and each mandatory attachment.
- **KNIC**: the filings adopting the ISO base homeowners forms currently used in Kentucky, all current KNIC-specific homeowners endorsements and Kentucky-specific amendatories used with those forms, and the current rate/rule/underwriting manuals to the extent not already public.
- **ISO / Insurance Services Office, Inc.**: the Kentucky homeowners filings for the ISO base forms and Kentucky HO endorsements you care about, especially **HO 00 03**, **HO 00 05**, and Kentucky homeowners endorsements that KNIC currently uses or may adopt, together with any DOI disposition identifying the currently approved edition and state applicability.
- For all three buckets: the **clean form PDFs**, the filing disposition, and enough filing metadata to connect the current in-force document to the approving filing history. If any responsive form text is withheld or redacted, ask DOI to **identify the specific exemption relied on** for each withheld document or redaction. citeturn46view0turn8view0turn43view0

What you should have ready before that call or email is: the full carrier legal names; the line of business (**homeowners**); any known form numbers; any known SERFF tracking numbers; the approximate date bands that matter; and a short statement that you want **existing electronic records only**, in the format they are maintained, for **internal compliance/audit review**. For KNIC, it will help to cite the public manual’s mandatory-form references so DOI can target missing clean texts instead of resending the public manual. For KFBM, because the base form number is unknown, ask DOI to identify the currently used jacket by querying the carrier’s currently approved homeowners program rather than by form-number search alone. citeturn27view0turn32view0turn12view0turn43view0

A practical template is:

> Please produce, in existing electronic format, the records sufficient to identify the **currently approved and currently in-use Kentucky homeowners policy package** for Kentucky Farm Bureau Mutual Insurance Company and Kentucky National Insurance Company, including base policy forms, all mandatory Kentucky amendatories and endorsements, current rule/rate manual pages, current underwriting manuals or guidelines, and the foundational approving filings and latest amending filings for each currently used form.  
>  
> For Kentucky National Insurance Company, please include the filings and clean copies for the current use of ISO homeowners base forms and KNIC-specific forms referenced in the current Kentucky homeowners manual, including Kentucky-specific endorsements used with all homeowners policies.  
>  
> For Kentucky Farm Bureau Mutual Insurance Company, please identify and produce the current homeowners base jacket and all endorsements attached to a standard owner-occupied Kentucky homeowners policy, together with current manuals and any pre-November 1, 2018 filings necessary to identify the currently used base form and endorsement stack.  
>  
> If any responsive records are withheld or redacted, please identify the specific statutory exemption for each withheld or redacted item.  
>  
> I request existing electronic records only and do not request the agency to create a new record or compile a new report.

That wording is broad enough to catch the package, but specific enough to avoid the classic “any and all” problem. It also tees up the redaction issue cleanly. citeturn43view0turn7view2turn46view0
